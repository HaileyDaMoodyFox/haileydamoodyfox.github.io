Linux, An OS that probably doesn't need an introduction, I personaly left from Windows 11 in 2024 to Linux. During that time I've had my ups and downs with Linux, Something I've grown to notice is how reliant Linux is on the internet, I've wondered if i could use Linux fully offline, That's where i found out about this tool named Jigdo.

Jigsaw Download (Jigdo)

Jigdo was originally designed by Richard Atterer in the 1991 (Though, He has since steped down, Giving over maintenace to Steve McIntyre), It's purpose was to make it easier for distributions to allow for the downloading of ISO files without needing to host the full ISO files on their servers. Jigdo uses a .jigdo file and a .template file, These files point to a repo, It tells Jigdo what to download and how to assemble them into an ISO locally on your PC, for personal use. My favorite use is burning to DVDs / putting on USB sticks for fully offline use.

Why i love Jigdo

Jigdo is my favorite for software preservation, I am a gal who loves retro software, It's always a treat of mine to look back on the past, And i think everybody should! I personally love to install older versions of Debian on VMs just to experience the past i never got to experence, I was born in the early 2000s, Meaning i missed out on a lot of cool tech and software, Well, Now i can experence it for the first time with a modern perspective!
